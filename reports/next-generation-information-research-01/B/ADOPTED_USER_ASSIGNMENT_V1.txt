NFL — Next-Generation Predictive Research: Dynamic Player Roles and Exact-Line Probabilities

Recipients: Existing Codex Sessions B and C

Priority: Research continuation, separate from the active production-release assignment.

Primary objective

Our long-term goal remains to identify methods capable of exceptionally accurate NFL player-prop Over/Under predictions across all twelve categories.

The immediate scientific objective is to discover genuinely new predictive information or modeling mechanisms that improve on the strongest existing A/B/C methods, measured on comparable actual offered lines.

We are not asking for another general algorithm tournament, additional historical averages, or a repeat of previously unsuccessful player-profile experiments.

We want to investigate whether upcoming player roles, teammate availability, opportunity redistribution, game context, and better probability distributions can materially improve predictions.

Accuracy on the actual Over/Under decision is the primary metric for recommendation products. Numerical forecast error, calibration, coverage and high-confidence selection accuracy remain separate measurements.

An 80–90% hit rate is an aspiration to test honestly, not a required outcome or permission to inflate probabilities.

1. Required first step: determine what has genuinely not been tested

Before fitting another model:

1. Read the latest existing A/B/C method inventories, experiments, audit findings and current source contracts.
2. Identify completed experiments involving player profiles, teammate availability, target share, defensive adjustments, workload allocation, seven-day versus shorter history, calibration, direct probabilities and market assistance.
3. Preserve their negative, inconclusive and positive findings.
4. Explain exactly how the proposed new experiment differs.
5. Determine which source observations actually existed before each historical decision.

Produce a short NEW-INFORMATION-FEASIBILITY receipt.

Classify proposed inputs as:

* Already tested.
* Equivalent information already available.
* Genuinely new and historically qualified.
* Available only prospectively.
* Unavailable or source-blocked.

Do not execute a redundant experiment.

If no new information can be qualified, report that result and identify the precise source limitation rather than manufacturing another model.

2. Session B: predictive QB opportunity model

Investigate whether we can improve the forecast distribution of a QB’s upcoming workload and conversion efficiency using genuinely pregame information about:

* Expected QB role and availability.
* Changes to the offensive line and receiving personnel.
* Historical pass/run tendencies under relevant personnel configurations.
* Upcoming team passing opportunity.
* Expected pass attempts and their uncertainty.
* Completion efficiency conditional on attempts and relevant passing situations.

The hypothesis is not that another recent-game average improves accuracy.

The hypothesis is that predicting meaningful changes in the upcoming offensive situation improves the existing conditional prediction.

Compare the strongest applicable retained method against the same method with one source-qualified new information block.

Previously completed B timing/profile experiments remain controls. Do not repeat them.

Preserve the separate evidence for attempts and completions, and enforce physically coherent probability support where the new procedure models both.

If the available information does not distinguish the proposed method from previously tested B procedures, stop that experiment and report why.

3. Session C: predictive receiving-role redistribution

Investigate whether we can forecast how targets and receiving opportunities are redistributed when the expected offensive personnel changes.

Potential qualifying information includes:

* Previously established target and route opportunity.
* Teammate availability and role changes.
* Expected participation and target competition.
* QB/player target-allocation history.
* Team passing opportunity.
* Source-qualified receiving alignment and play-design information.
* Uncertainty in the player’s upcoming target share.

The hypothesis is that personnel-conditioned redistribution predicts the next-game target distribution better than the retained historical profile alone.

Do not use target-game actual workload, realized alignment, future injury information or unavailable route data.

Do not assume WR1/WR2, X/Z labels or snap share establish the player’s exact pregame target distribution.

Use the existing C P1 reception method as a named control and compare with the strongest compatible retained A procedure.

The existing target-share, contextual-profile, timing and defense experiments are completed evidence. Do not rerun those recipes under new names.

If no qualified teammate-availability or role-change source supports a distinct test, report the missing information precisely.

4. Statistical design and execution

For each session, select one genuinely new, source-qualified hypothesis at a time.

When the feasibility receipt establishes a distinct hypothesis, complete the bounded comparison under existing authorized budgets and execution controls.

Use the unchanged strong retained method as the baseline. If the new package introduces additional missingness or coverage indicators, distinguish their effect from the substantive predictive values where practical.

Keep matched:

* Player/game/category and role.
* Historical information cutoff.
* Actual offered line and market contract.
* Scored outcomes.
* Eligibility and participation rules.
* Training/evaluation chronology.
* Evaluation population.
* Complete fallback and selection policies.

Report each candidate’s own Over wins/losses, Under wins/losses, combined hit rate, coverage, unavailable predictions, abstentions and uncertainty.

Also report appropriate numerical and probabilistic scores, including Brier/log loss where valid.

Compare against strong constant-side, retained learned and qualified market controls—not only the candidate’s weakest baseline.

Use earlier information for fitting and tuning. Preserve later untouched evaluations for confirmation. Do not optimize the tested method against the same outcomes used to claim independent superiority.

A five-scored-week result may satisfy the existing primary-product duration floor, but does not automatically justify deployment or establish future accuracy.

5. Additional research priorities — retained backlog

After the first bounded experiments, assess the following in this priority order without launching them all at once.

Priority 2 — Market-anchored exact-line probabilities

Compare qualified contemporaneous market reference probabilities, football-only probabilities, and a regularized market-plus-football correction.

Keep same-book-assisted and independent-peer-book models correctly labeled. Exclude the evaluated bookmaker from a reference claimed to be independent.

Require valid market definitions and observation clocks. No paid data acquisition or invented historical odds.

Priority 3 — Coherent game-to-player opportunity model

Investigate one structurally coherent method linking team opportunities, player allocation, efficiency, and category outcomes.

Preserve constraints such as completions no greater than attempts, receptions no greater than targets, coherent combined statistics, genuine zero-workload possibilities and plausible tail support.

This must be a changed probability model—not merely more Monte Carlo draws from the same distribution.

Priority 4 — Play-design and matchup interactions

Investigate source-qualified play design, target depth, defensive alignment and opponent-adjusted efficiency when these provide new information beyond existing defense features.

The supplied defensive spreadsheet is not approved as a historical input. Its November 9/10 values must not be backfilled into Weeks 1–8.

Priority 5 — A genuinely missing probability-model family

Review whether NGBoost, distributional forests, XGBoostLSS or related approaches still lack completed qualified testing.

Choose a family only when its distributional assumption or estimation mechanism addresses a specific unresolved weakness.

Do not run a broad algorithm search merely because a library has not appeared in an earlier report.

6. High-accuracy selection research

Keep two distinct goals:

Broad-board accuracy: Improve predictions across a meaningful share of compatible offered opportunities.

Selective accuracy: Determine whether a frozen, predeclared procedure can identify a smaller group of genuinely higher-probability decisions.

For selective research, report selected volume, eligible volume, Over/Under split, game/week coverage, actual accuracy and the relationship between stated probabilities and observed correctness.

Do not select favorable historical subgroups after seeing the results and present them as prospective success.

A procedure producing zero selections does not achieve 90% accuracy. A tiny group of successful selections does not establish broad or dependable weekly performance.
